# Sleeping

Right-click Strawberry → **Sleep after inactivity**: 1, 5 (default), 10, or 30 minutes, or Never. **Sleep now** previews it immediately when she is idle. Leave the mouse still after selecting it; keyboard or mouse activity wakes her.

The timer measures desktop-wide input inactivity and time since the last widget activity. Music, listening, thinking, talking, visible speech, reactions, dragging, and an open menu prevent sleep. After music stops or a conversation finishes, a fresh delay starts. The preference is saved in `user://widget.cfg` as `[sleep] after_minutes`; fractional values are accepted there, and zero disables automatic sleep.

## The tuck and spin

Going to sleep takes 2.6 seconds. She crouches (0.22 s) and hops, about 0.11 above where she stands. In the air her legs and claws fold tight and shrink in under the shell until they are hidden, her eye stalks bend outward at the base and droop over the rim, and her lids close. She lands right side up, low on her belly, with a squash that rings out. Then she turns once around (one full 360°), fast at first and slowing to a stop, while the shell wobbles like a coin settling on a table: it tilts up to about 10°, the low side runs round the rim twice in the one turn, and the tilt dies away with the turn. She ends facing the user. Asleep, only the shell and the drooped, shut eyes show; `sleep_loop` breathes (eight seconds, a longer exhale) and the eyes ride the breath.

Waking takes 1.5 seconds and is the reverse: a shiver (0.35 s) with a peek, a crouch, a hop with a small twist (about 70° and back), everything pops out a little past its place and settles, the lids open, and she lands on her feet with a small bounce before the normal clip takes over again.

The movement is `widget/sleep_tuck.gd`, a procedural layer on top of the clip, the same way the reactions and dance styles work. The pose is a few channels: the body's lift and squash, how far the legs and claws are tucked, how far the eyes droop, the lids, the turn, the coin tilt and the shiver. Each sequence is a function of time into those channels. The controller (`sleep_controller.gd`, process priority 80) runs the clock; the layer writes the bones at 163, after the leg solve (159) and the pincers (162), before `turn.gd` (165), the gaze (170) and the hat (180). The squash pivots on her belly's lowest point. The turn and the tilt are a rotation of the skeleton's `root` bone about the belly, lifted so its lowest point stays on the desk. The eye stalks bend at their own base, where they leave the shell (the `eyestalk_L/R` bones' heads are there, so the rig needed no new bones), and the base slides a little out and down the dome as they bend so the stalk inside the shell stays hidden. Legs and claws fold and shrink with bone scale. A part that no clip key writes (the eye stalks' position) is taken from before the layer each frame, so the layer never stacks on itself.

**Interruptions.** Any input wakes her at once, wherever she is. Caught in the crouch she just stands back up. Caught in the air or mid-turn she drops into the wake's crouch and hops up from there. *Sleep now* while she is getting up takes her down again: still mostly tucked, she drops back and settles from the landing; otherwise from the crouch. A new sequence blends from the channels she is in over 0.3 s, and an unfinished turn unwinds to the nearest full turn over 0.8 s, so nothing snaps and nothing is left half way. *Sleep now* does nothing while a performance is waiting for her to get up.

**Other layers.** `turn.gd` hands its drift, glances and the layers' turns over to the sequence (eased over 0.25 s) whenever she is not awake, and takes them back afterwards; only the place on the monitor stays. The gaze is off from the first crouch until she is up. Dancing, reactions, touch reactions and walking never run while she is not awake (`can_sleep`, `wander.gd`, `touch.gd`). The hat follows the shell through the hop and the turn, and rests.

**Reduced motion.** Where the system reports the reduced-motion setting (Godot's `DisplayServer.accessibility_should_reduce_animation()`: Windows and macOS; on Linux it is unknown and she hops), she folds and settles in place instead: no hop, no turn, no wobble, no shiver (1.6 s down, 1.2 s up).

**The window.** The hop and the turn stay well inside her window (the top of the hop is about 33 px up; she starts in the lower part of a 380 × 560 window). On Linux the click-through shape is the hull of her meshes at rest, so it does not follow the hop: for the fraction of a second her eyes may be above it, a click there passes to the desktop. Asleep she is inside it, so a poke on her shell still reaches her. On Windows the window region follows her posed bones (WIRING.md §13), the hop and the shrunk legs included.

Music or a notification wakes her before its performance starts; newer incoming performances replace older pending ones during that short transition.

Sleep is a local widget state, so the daemon's existing idle/dancing contract is unchanged. The GLB still has `sleep_enter`, `sleep_loop` and `wake_up`; the widget plays only `sleep_loop` now (for the breath and the shut lids). While she goes down and gets up, the lids go through the blink controller's `sleep` layer; asleep the clip owns them. Her voice still opens the pincers (`pincer_L/R`).

## Inactivity

Desktop inactivity is queried once per second on a worker thread through `desktop_idle.py`. X11 uses XScreenSaver; GNOME Wayland uses Mutter's idle monitor; Windows uses `GetLastInputInfo`. Only elapsed idle time is read, never key contents. If the API is unavailable, automatic sleep is disabled rather than mistaking activity in another app for inactivity. Other Wayland desktops need their own idle adapter. On Linux the helper runs on the system Python 3 (`/usr/bin/python3`) with the desktop's existing X11 libraries; Windows has no system Python, so `strawberry widget` and the tray pass their own interpreter in `STRAWBERRY_PYTHON`, and without it automatic sleep stays off. The exported binary carries the helper and copies it out to the cache dir to run it.

## Updating and checks

After updating: right-click → **Restart widget**. The new menu appears after restart.

Validation: `godot --headless --audio-driver Dummy --path widget --script res://validate_sleep.gd` (also in `scripts/check_phase1.sh`) uses isolated settings and no daemon connection. It checks the delay and the state blockers; the hop, the legs and claws tucked away in the air, one full turn with a wobble, ending facing the user, the drooped shut eyes, the breath and the resting hat; the wake back to the normal pose with a hop and a small twist; interruptions at seven points on the way down and three on the way up, each ending in a finished state with no frame sharper than the uninterrupted landings (the largest change of step from one frame to the next of the eyes and the body); `turn.gd` handing over; the reduced-motion version; that she stays inside the window and the click-through shape; music and notification wake-up; and persistence. Add `-- --capture-dir=/absolute/path` and run with a display (`xvfb-run -a` works) to capture key frames of going to sleep and waking. Blender export and clean re-import reports are in `v2/export_checks.json` and `v2/reimport_checks.json`.
